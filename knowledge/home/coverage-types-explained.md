# Homeowners Coverage Types Explained

A standard homeowners policy is built from several distinct coverage parts, each protecting a different aspect of your property and financial life. Understanding how these parts fit together helps you see what is protected, what limits apply, and where gaps may exist. The most common structure uses labeled coverages, often referred to as Coverage A through Coverage F, so that adjusters and policyholders can talk about each piece precisely.

## Dwelling (Coverage A)

Dwelling coverage, commonly labeled Coverage A, pays to repair or rebuild the physical structure of your home when it is damaged by a covered peril. This includes the walls, roof, foundation, floors, and anything permanently attached to the house such as built-in appliances, plumbing, wiring, and heating systems. The dwelling limit should reflect the cost to rebuild the home with similar materials and workmanship, which is different from the home's market value or the price you paid for it. Rebuilding cost can be higher or lower than market value because it excludes the land but includes current labor and material prices.

Because construction costs change over time, many insurers include an inflation adjustment that raises the dwelling limit at each renewal. If you make major improvements such as adding a room, finishing a basement, or upgrading to premium finishes, you should increase your dwelling limit so the higher rebuild cost is fully covered. Being underinsured on Coverage A is one of the most common and costly mistakes a homeowner can make after a total loss.

## Other Structures (Coverage B)

Other structures coverage, usually Coverage B, protects buildings and installations on your property that are not attached to the main house. Typical examples include a detached garage, a storage shed, a fence, a gazebo, a guest cottage, or an in-ground swimming pool. This coverage is normally set as a percentage of the dwelling limit, often around ten percent, though you can request a higher amount if you have valuable detached structures. If you run a business out of a detached structure, standard coverage may be limited or excluded, so a separate endorsement or commercial policy may be needed.

## Personal Property (Coverage C)

Personal property coverage, typically Coverage C, pays for your belongings when they are stolen or damaged by a covered peril, including furniture, clothing, electronics, and kitchen items. This coverage often follows your possessions off the premises, so items stolen from your car or from a hotel room while traveling may still be covered up to a limit. Certain categories such as jewelry, watches, firearms, and cash carry special sublimits that cap how much the policy pays, even though the overall personal property limit is much higher. To fully protect high-value items, you can add scheduled personal property, sometimes called a rider or floater, which insures listed items for their appraised value.

## Loss of Use (Coverage D)

Loss of use coverage, generally Coverage D, helps pay the extra costs of living somewhere else while your home is being repaired after a covered loss. This is often called additional living expense and can cover hotel bills, temporary rentals, restaurant meals above your normal grocery budget, and even pet boarding in some cases. The coverage reimburses the difference between your normal cost of living and the higher cost you face while displaced, not your entire new expense. Loss of use is usually capped at a percentage of the dwelling limit or limited to a set number of months, so keeping receipts and working closely with your adjuster helps ensure timely reimbursement.

## Personal Liability and Medical Payments (Coverage E and F)

Personal liability coverage, usually Coverage E, protects you financially if you are found legally responsible for injuring someone or damaging their property, whether the incident happens at your home or elsewhere. It can pay for the injured party's damages as well as your legal defense costs, up to the policy limit you select. Medical payments coverage, often Coverage F, is a smaller no-fault benefit that pays medical bills for guests hurt on your property regardless of who was at fault, which can help resolve minor incidents without a lawsuit. Because a serious liability claim can exceed standard limits, many homeowners raise their liability limit or add a separate umbrella policy for extra protection.
