# How Long Claims Take and How to Challenge a Decision

Claim handling follows a general rhythm, though the exact timing depends on the complexity of the loss and how quickly information is exchanged. Knowing the typical stages helps you set expectations and recognize when to follow up. Timelines are estimates for education, not guarantees, and specific deadlines in your policy or in local regulation always take precedence.

## Typical Claim Timeline

After you file an FNOL, an adjuster is usually assigned within one business day. The adjuster then acknowledges the claim, gathers documentation, and inspects the loss if needed, which can take several days to a few weeks depending on availability and the nature of the damage. Once the adjuster has enough information, a coverage decision is made and, if approved, payment is issued according to the settlement terms. Straightforward claims can close in a couple of weeks, while complex losses involving disputes, multiple parties, or extensive damage take longer.

## What Can Slow a Claim Down

Delays most often come from missing documentation, difficulty scheduling an inspection, disputes about the cause or value of a loss, or the involvement of third parties whose information is needed. You can help keep a claim on schedule by responding to requests quickly, providing complete estimates, and keeping your contact information current. If a claim stalls, asking the adjuster what specific item is outstanding usually identifies the bottleneck.

## Disputing a Claim Decision

If you disagree with how a claim was handled or with the amount offered, you can dispute the decision. A dispute opens a review by a licensed claims representative who was not solely responsible for the original decision. Explaining clearly why you disagree and providing supporting evidence, such as an independent repair estimate or additional photos, strengthens a dispute. Filing a dispute does not by itself change the outcome; it triggers a fresh review of the facts and the policy terms.

## Appeals and Escalation

If a dispute does not resolve the disagreement, many insurers offer a further appeal or a formal review, and independent options such as appraisal, mediation, or filing a complaint with a regulator may be available depending on the product and region. In an appraisal, each side selects an appraiser and an impartial umpire helps settle a difference over the amount of a loss. Throughout, you have the right to a clear explanation of the basis for a decision. An assistant can open a dispute and escalate it to a human representative, but it cannot overturn a decision or guarantee any particular result.
