# What to Report When You File a Claim

The first notice of loss, or FNOL, is the report that opens a claim. Every FNOL captures a shared core of information regardless of the product involved: the policy number, the date and time the loss occurred, the location, a plain description of what happened, and the best way to reach you. Providing accurate contact details matters because an adjuster often needs to follow up within the first business day. Filing an FNOL starts a review of the loss; it is not a determination that the loss is covered or that a payment will be made.

Beyond the core details, each product asks for information specific to the kind of loss it covers. Gathering the right details before you file helps the adjuster evaluate the claim faster and reduces the number of follow-up requests. If you do not have every detail immediately, it is better to file promptly with what you know than to delay, because many policies expect prompt notice of a loss.

## Auto Claims

For an auto claim, be ready to describe how the accident happened, the direction of travel, and the point of impact on each vehicle. Report the drivers and passengers involved, whether anyone was injured, and whether emergency services or police responded. If a police report was taken, the report number helps the adjuster obtain the official record. Note the other party's insurance and vehicle information when the loss involved another driver, along with the names and contact details of any witnesses.

## Homeowners and Renters Claims

For property claims such as fire, water, wind, or theft, describe the cause of the damage, the rooms or areas affected, and the approximate extent of the loss. Note the date the damage was discovered, which can differ from the date it began for slow leaks or hidden damage. For theft or vandalism, a police report is typically expected. Take steps to prevent further damage, such as shutting off water or covering a broken window, and keep receipts for any emergency measures because reasonable protective costs are often reimbursable.

## Life Insurance Claims

A life insurance claim is usually filed by a named beneficiary after the insured person has died. The FNOL captures the policy number, the insured's full name and date of death, and the cause of death when known. A certified copy of the death certificate is the central document, and the insurer verifies the beneficiary's identity before releasing any benefit. Because these claims are sensitive, the process emphasizes clear communication and confirming that the person filing is entitled to the benefit.

## Health Claims

Many health claims are submitted directly by a provider, but a member may file when paying out of pocket or seeking reimbursement. The FNOL captures the date of service, the provider, the type of care received, and the amount billed. Itemized bills and any explanation of benefits already received help the reviewer match the claim to covered services and apply the correct cost sharing.

## Commercial and Umbrella Claims

Commercial claims vary widely by the exposure involved, such as property damage to a business location, a liability claim from a third party, or a business interruption. The FNOL captures the nature of the loss, the affected operations, and whether a third party has made a demand or filed suit. An umbrella claim generally comes into play when a loss exceeds the limits of an underlying auto or property policy, so the FNOL references the underlying claim and the primary carrier handling it.
