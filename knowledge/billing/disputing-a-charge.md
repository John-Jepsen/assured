# Disputing a Charge

If a charge on your account looks wrong, the first step is to identify exactly what you are questioning, because different problems have different resolutions. A charge may be higher than expected because of a legitimate premium change at renewal, an added installment fee, a state surcharge, or a past-due balance carried forward, and none of those is a billing error even though they raise the amount you owe. A true billing error is something like a duplicate charge, a payment that was made but not recorded, an incorrect amount, or a fee applied to the wrong policy. Sorting a genuine error from an expected increase saves time and points you to the right path.

Before opening a formal dispute, it helps to review your recent statements and payment history so you can describe the specific charge, its date, and why you believe it is incorrect. An assistant can walk through your current invoice line by line and explain what each item represents once your identity is verified. In many cases this review resolves the confusion on its own, because the charge turns out to be a documented fee, tax, or premium adjustment rather than a mistake. If the explanation confirms the charge is correct, no dispute is needed, though you can still ask about ways to lower future costs.

When a review does not resolve the issue and the charge still appears to be an error, the matter moves to a formal dispute. The assistant does not have authority to reverse charges, waive fees, or issue credits on its own, because those actions require judgment and account changes that are reserved for licensed staff. Instead, the assistant gathers the relevant details, confirms your identity and ownership of the policy, and escalates the dispute to a licensed representative. This escalation path exists so that any adjustment to your account is made deliberately and correctly, with a full record of what changed and why.

During an escalation, a representative can investigate the disputed charge against your payment records and your policy's filed rates and fees, and then take the appropriate action if an error is confirmed. That action might be a correction, a credit, a refund, or a written explanation of why the charge stands. Timelines for resolving a dispute depend on your insurer and the complexity of the issue, and you may be asked to provide supporting information such as proof of a payment you believe was missed. Keeping records of your payments makes this process faster and smoother.

Throughout a dispute, the assistant will only phrase facts that have been verified against your account, so it will not invent a credit, promise a specific outcome, or tell you a charge was reversed unless that reversal has actually occurred. If you are ever unsure whether a payment posted or a fee was removed, ask for your current billing status rather than assuming. This careful approach protects you from acting on incorrect information and ensures that a real error gets to the person who can fix it.
