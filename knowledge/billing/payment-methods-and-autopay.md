# Payment Methods and Autopay

Most insurers accept several ways to pay your premium, and choosing the right one can affect both convenience and cost. Common options include electronic funds transfer (EFT) directly from a checking or savings account, debit or credit card payments, and one-time online payments through a secure customer portal. Some insurers also support payment by mail with a paper check or by phone through an automated system. The specific methods available to you depend on your insurer, your state, and the type of policy you hold.

Autopay, sometimes called automatic payment or recurring billing, authorizes your insurer to withdraw your premium on a set schedule so you never miss a due date. When you enroll, you pick a funding source such as a bank account or card, and the insurer charges it automatically on each billing date. Autopay reduces the risk of a lapse caused by a forgotten payment, and many insurers offer a small discount for enrolling in bank-account autopay because it lowers their processing costs and default risk.

Enrolling in or changing autopay is an account change that requires identity verification because it touches your payment information. A customer service assistant can explain how autopay works and what it would cost, but it will confirm your identity before making any change to your payment method or schedule. No full card or bank account numbers are stored in chat transcripts, and sensitive details are handled through secure, tokenized systems rather than plain text.

If an autopay withdrawal is declined, most insurers send a notice and attempt the charge again after a short waiting period, or they place the bill back into manual status so you can pay another way. It is a good practice to keep your funding source up to date, especially after a card expires or a bank account changes, so an automatic payment does not fail unexpectedly. If you are unsure whether a payment went through, the assistant can review your billing status once your identity is verified.

You can typically switch payment methods at any time before your next due date without penalty. Changing from monthly to a less frequent schedule, such as quarterly or annual, may reduce or eliminate installment fees that some insurers charge for spreading payments across the year. The assistant can describe the trade-offs, but it does not set fees or rates and will escalate to a licensed representative if you have questions specific to your policy.
