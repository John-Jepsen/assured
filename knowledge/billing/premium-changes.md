# Why Your Premium May Change

Insurance premiums can change at renewal for several reasons. Understanding the
common drivers helps explain an increase, though your specific change depends on
your policy and your insurer's filed rates.

Common reasons an auto premium increases: a recent at-fault accident or claim,
adding a vehicle or driver, a change in your garaging location or ZIP code,
industry-wide rate changes approved by your state's insurance department, loss of a
discount (for example, a paperless or safe-driver discount lapsing), or a change in
annual mileage or vehicle use.

For homeowners policies, premiums often rise due to increased rebuilding costs
(inflation in materials and labor), prior claims, changes to the dwelling's insured
value, or regional catastrophe risk.

A premium change is not the same as a billing error. If you believe a charge is
incorrect, the assistant can review your billing status and, if needed, escalate to
a licensed representative. The assistant does not set rates and cannot guarantee a
specific premium.
