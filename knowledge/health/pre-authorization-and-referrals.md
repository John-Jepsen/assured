# Pre-Authorization, Prior Approval, and Referrals

Some health plans require a review or an approval step before certain services are provided or before the plan will pay its share. These steps are commonly called pre-authorization, prior authorization, or precertification, and they are separate from the referrals that some plans require to see a specialist. Understanding when these steps apply can help members avoid unexpected costs and delays. The services that require approval differ by plan, so members should always check their own plan rules and work with their provider to complete any required steps.

## What Pre-Authorization Means

Pre-authorization is a process in which the plan reviews a proposed service, medication, or procedure to determine whether it meets the plan's criteria for coverage before the care is delivered. It is often required for higher-cost or non-emergency services such as certain imaging, planned hospital stays, some surgeries, and specific medications. Pre-authorization is a coverage and payment review; it is not a medical decision about what care a person needs, which remains between the patient and their treating provider. Receiving pre-authorization confirms the plan's review outcome for coverage purposes but does not itself guarantee a final paid amount, since other plan terms still apply.

## Who Requests It and How

In most cases, the treating provider's office submits the pre-authorization request to the plan on the member's behalf, along with supporting clinical information. The plan then reviews the request and responds with an approval, a denial, or a request for more information, usually within a timeframe described in the plan documents. Members can help by confirming with their provider that any required authorization has been requested before a scheduled service. If a request is denied, plans generally offer an appeals process that allows the member or provider to ask for the decision to be reviewed again.

## Referrals

A referral is a recommendation or authorization from one provider, often a primary care physician, for a member to see another provider such as a specialist. Referrals are most common in HMO-style plans, where seeing a specialist without one may mean the visit is not covered. A referral is different from a pre-authorization: a referral concerns which provider a member sees, while a pre-authorization concerns whether a specific service will be covered. Some plans require both a referral and a pre-authorization for the same episode of care, depending on the situation.

## Why These Steps Matter for Cost

Skipping a required pre-authorization or referral can lead to reduced coverage or a claim being denied, leaving the member responsible for a larger share or the full cost. Because requirements vary so much between plans and services, confirming the rules in advance is one of the most effective ways to avoid surprise expenses. Emergency care is generally handled under separate rules, and members should never delay emergency treatment to seek approval first. This document explains general processes only and does not describe any specific plan's requirements; members should consult their plan documents or contact their insurer to confirm what applies to a particular service.
