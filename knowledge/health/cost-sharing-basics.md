# Understanding How You and Your Health Plan Share Costs

Most health insurance plans use a system called cost-sharing, which means the plan and the member both pay a portion of covered medical expenses. Four terms describe how these shared costs work: the deductible, coinsurance, copays, and the out-of-pocket maximum. Understanding how these pieces fit together helps members anticipate what they may owe for a given service. The exact amounts vary by plan, so members should always check their own plan documents for the figures that apply to them.

## The Deductible

The deductible is the amount a member pays for covered services before the plan begins to pay its share. For example, if a plan has a $1,500 deductible, the member generally pays the first $1,500 of covered care each plan year, and then the plan starts contributing. Some services, such as preventive care or certain visits, may be covered before the deductible is met, depending on the plan design. Family plans often have both an individual deductible and a higher family deductible, and the way these interact differs from plan to plan.

## Coinsurance

Coinsurance is the percentage of a covered service that a member pays after the deductible has been met. A common arrangement is an 80/20 split, where the plan pays 80 percent of the allowed amount and the member pays the remaining 20 percent. Coinsurance is based on the plan's allowed or negotiated amount for a service, not necessarily the provider's full billed charge. Because it is a percentage rather than a flat fee, the member's coinsurance cost rises with the price of the service.

## Copays

A copay, or copayment, is a fixed dollar amount a member pays for a specific service at the time of care, such as a set fee for an office visit or a prescription. Unlike coinsurance, a copay does not change based on the total cost of the service. Some plans apply copays before the deductible is met, while others apply them only after; the plan documents describe which rule applies. Copays for different service types, such as primary care, specialists, and emergency visits, are often set at different amounts.

## The Out-of-Pocket Maximum

The out-of-pocket maximum is the most a member has to pay for covered services in a plan year. Once the member's spending on deductibles, coinsurance, and copays reaches this limit, the plan generally pays 100 percent of covered, in-network services for the rest of the year. Monthly premiums do not count toward the out-of-pocket maximum, and charges for services the plan does not cover typically do not count either. This limit is designed to protect members from unlimited medical costs in a year with serious or ongoing care.

## How the Pieces Work Together

These four elements usually apply in a sequence over the course of a plan year. A member first pays toward the deductible, then shares costs through coinsurance or copays, and finally reaches the out-of-pocket maximum, after which the plan covers the rest. Because plan designs differ widely, the same medical service can cost different amounts under different plans. This document explains general concepts only and does not describe the terms of any specific plan; members should review their own benefit summary or contact their insurer for the amounts that apply to their coverage.
