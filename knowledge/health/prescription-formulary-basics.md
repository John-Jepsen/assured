# Understanding a Prescription Drug Formulary

A formulary is the list of prescription medications that a health plan covers, along with the rules and cost-sharing that apply to each one. Plans use formularies to organize covered drugs and to guide how members and the plan share the cost of prescriptions. Because formularies are set by each plan and can change over time, a medication's coverage and cost can differ from one plan to another. Members should check their own plan's current formulary or contact their insurer to confirm how a specific medication is covered.

## Drug Tiers

Most formularies group covered medications into tiers, and each tier is associated with a different level of member cost-sharing. A common structure places lower-cost generic drugs on the lowest tier, preferred brand-name drugs on a middle tier, and non-preferred or specialty drugs on higher tiers with greater cost-sharing. The tier a drug falls into affects whether the member pays a copay or coinsurance and how much that amount is. Members can often lower their costs by discussing tier placement and covered alternatives with their prescriber and pharmacist.

## Generic, Brand-Name, and Specialty Drugs

Generic drugs contain the same active ingredients as their brand-name counterparts and are typically placed on lower cost-sharing tiers. Brand-name drugs may be listed as preferred or non-preferred, which influences their tier and the member's share of the cost. Specialty drugs, often used for complex or chronic conditions, are usually on the highest tiers and may carry additional handling or pharmacy requirements. Whether a specific drug is covered and at what tier depends entirely on the individual plan's formulary.

## Common Formulary Rules

Formularies often include utilization management rules that apply to certain medications. Prior authorization may be required, meaning the plan reviews the prescription before agreeing to cover it. Step therapy may ask that a member try one preferred medication before the plan covers an alternative, and quantity limits may cap how much of a drug is covered within a set period. These rules are coverage and payment tools; decisions about which medication is appropriate for a patient remain between the patient and their prescriber.

## When a Drug Is Not on the Formulary

If a medication is not on a plan's formulary, the plan may not cover it, or it may be covered only under specific circumstances. Many plans offer an exception or appeals process through which a member or prescriber can request coverage for a non-formulary drug when there is a supporting reason. Using an in-network pharmacy and asking the pharmacist about covered alternatives can also help members manage costs. This document explains general formulary concepts only and does not provide medical advice or describe any specific plan; members should consult their prescriber for medication decisions and their insurer or plan documents for coverage details.
