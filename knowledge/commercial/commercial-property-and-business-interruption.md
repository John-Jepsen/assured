# Commercial Property and Business Interruption Coverage

Commercial property insurance protects the physical assets a business relies on to operate. Covered property usually includes the building itself, if the business owns it, along with business personal property such as furniture, equipment, inventory, and tools. Many policies also extend to property of others that is temporarily in the business's care, and to improvements a tenant has made to a leased space. The goal of this coverage is to help a business repair, rebuild, or replace damaged property after a covered event so operations can resume.

Property policies are written on either a named-perils or an open-perils basis, and the difference matters when a claim is filed. A named-perils policy covers only the specific causes of loss listed in the policy, such as fire, windstorm, hail, or theft. An open-perils policy, sometimes called all-risk, covers any cause of loss that is not specifically excluded, which generally provides broader protection. In both forms, common exclusions include flood, earthquake, wear and tear, and intentional damage, and separate coverage is usually required to insure against excluded perils.

The way a claim is valued has a large effect on how much a business recovers. Replacement cost coverage pays to repair or replace damaged property with new property of similar kind and quality, without deducting for depreciation. Actual cash value coverage pays replacement cost minus depreciation, which reflects the used condition of the property at the time of loss. Replacement cost coverage typically costs more but leaves a smaller gap between the claim payment and the true cost to recover, which is why it is often recommended for essential equipment.

Business interruption coverage, sometimes called business income coverage, addresses the income a business loses when a covered property loss forces it to suspend or reduce operations. Rather than paying for physical damage, it replaces the net income the business would have earned and helps cover continuing expenses such as rent, loan payments, and payroll during the recovery period. Because a fire or major storm can shut a business down for weeks or months, this coverage often protects against a larger financial hit than the property damage itself.

Business interruption coverage usually includes a waiting period and a period of restoration that define when and how long benefits are paid. The waiting period, sometimes called a time deductible, is a set number of hours after the loss before coverage begins. The period of restoration is the window during which lost income is covered, generally lasting until the property is repaired and operations could reasonably resume. Some policies add extra expense coverage, which pays the added costs of operating from a temporary location so the business can keep serving customers while repairs are underway.
