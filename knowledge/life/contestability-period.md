# Contestability Period and Claim Review

The contestability period is a defined window, commonly the first two years after a life insurance policy takes effect, during which the insurer retains the right to investigate and potentially challenge a claim. Its purpose is to protect the insurance pool against fraud and material misrepresentation made on the application. During this period, if the insured passes away, the insurer may review the original application against the facts to confirm that the information provided was accurate and complete. Understanding how the contestability period works helps policyholders provide accurate information up front and set realistic expectations about claim timing.

## What Happens During the Contestability Period

If a death occurs while the policy is still within its contestability period, the insurer may conduct a closer review of the claim before paying the death benefit. This review can include comparing the application answers to medical records, prescription history, and other documentation to check for material misstatements about health, tobacco use, occupation, or hobbies. If the insurer finds that a material misrepresentation would have changed the underwriting decision, it may adjust the benefit, deny the claim, or rescind the policy and return the premiums paid. Honest and accurate answers on the original application are the best protection against a contested claim.

It is important to note that the contestability period does not mean every claim in the first two years is denied. Most claims are paid promptly; the period simply preserves the insurer's right to investigate when there is reason to question the application. A claim based on truthful, complete information typically clears review without issue, even if it falls within the contestable window. After the contestability period ends, the insurer generally cannot contest the policy for misstatements on the application, except in limited cases such as proven fraud where the law allows.

## Related Provisions

Life insurance policies also commonly include a suicide clause, typically covering the same initial period, under which the death benefit may be limited to a return of premiums if the insured dies by suicide within that window. This provision, like the contestability clause, is designed to discourage the purchase of coverage in bad faith and applies only during the stated period. A separate incontestability clause reinforces that, once the contestability period passes, the policy becomes far more difficult to challenge, giving long-term policyholders greater certainty that their beneficiaries will receive the benefit. Reinstating a lapsed policy can restart a new contestability period for the reinstated coverage, so policyholders should be aware that letting a policy lapse and later restoring it may reopen the insurer's right to contest.
