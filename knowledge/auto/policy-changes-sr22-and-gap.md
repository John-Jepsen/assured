# Vehicle and Driver Changes, SR-22 Basics, and Gap Coverage

Auto policies are not static; they change as your household, vehicles, and legal circumstances evolve. Knowing how to add or remove a car or driver, what an SR-22 filing means, and how gap coverage protects a financed vehicle helps you keep your policy accurate and your protection intact. Keeping your insurer informed of changes is important because gaps or errors can leave you underinsured or in violation of policy terms. This guide explains these common policy adjustments in plain language.

## Adding or Removing a Vehicle

When you buy, sell, or trade a vehicle, you should update your policy promptly so the correct car is covered. Adding a vehicle typically requires its identification number, year, make, model, and details about how it will be used and where it is kept. New vehicles often have a short automatic coverage window under an existing policy, but that grace period is limited, so notifying your insurer quickly is the safest approach. Removing a vehicle you no longer own stops you from paying for coverage you do not need and keeps your policy accurate.

## Adding or Removing a Driver

Insurers generally expect every licensed household member who regularly drives your vehicles to be listed on the policy. You may add a driver when a teen earns a license, a partner moves in, or another relative begins using your car. Removing a driver is appropriate when someone moves out, obtains their own policy, or no longer has access to your vehicles. Because each driver's age and record affect the premium, keeping the driver list current ensures accurate pricing and prevents claim disputes over unlisted drivers.

## SR-22 Basics

An SR-22 is not an insurance policy but a certificate your insurer files with the state to confirm you carry at least the minimum required liability coverage. It is typically required after serious violations such as driving without insurance, a major moving offense, or license reinstatement following a suspension. Once requested, your insurer submits the form and must notify the state if your policy lapses or is canceled, which is why maintaining continuous coverage is essential during the filing period. An SR-22 requirement usually lasts a set number of years, after which it can be removed once the state confirms it is no longer needed.

## Gap Coverage

Gap coverage, short for guaranteed asset protection, addresses the difference between what you owe on a financed or leased vehicle and its actual cash value if it is totaled or stolen. Because vehicles depreciate quickly, a loan balance can exceed the car's market value, especially in the early years of ownership. Without gap coverage, a total loss settlement based on actual cash value might not fully pay off your loan, leaving you responsible for the remainder. This coverage is most valuable for drivers with small down payments, long loan terms, or vehicles that depreciate rapidly.

## Keeping Your Policy Current

Each of these changes shares a common theme: an accurate policy protects you when you need to file a claim. Delaying an update, whether it is a new car, a new driver, or a required filing, can create coverage gaps or pricing errors that surface at the worst possible moment. Reviewing your policy after any major life or vehicle change, and confirming that required filings remain active, keeps your protection reliable. When in doubt, contacting your insurer to verify that a change is properly recorded is a simple safeguard.
