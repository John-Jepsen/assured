# Roadside Assistance, Towing, and Glass Coverage

Beyond the core coverages that handle accidents, many policies offer add-ons that address everyday breakdowns and minor damage. Roadside assistance, towing, and glass coverage are optional features that make owning and operating a vehicle more convenient. They tend to have modest premiums because individual claims are usually small, yet they can save you time, stress, and out-of-pocket cost during an inconvenient situation. Understanding what each option includes helps you decide whether the added convenience is worth the extra cost.

## Roadside Assistance

Roadside assistance provides help when your vehicle becomes disabled, whether from a mechanical failure or a common inconvenience. Typical services include jump-starting a dead battery, changing a flat tire, delivering fuel when you run out, and unlocking a car when keys are locked inside. Coverage usually pays for the service call up to a set limit, and some plans include a certain number of service events per year. This add-on is popular with drivers who want a dependable point of contact whenever their vehicle will not start or move.

## Towing and Labor Coverage

Towing and labor coverage pays to move a disabled vehicle to a repair facility and may cover on-site labor performed at the breakdown location. Policies often set a per-tow dollar limit or a mileage cap, so it is helpful to know how far your vehicle can be towed before extra charges apply. This coverage is distinct from the towing that may be included after a covered collision, which is typically handled under your collision or comprehensive claim. For drivers with older vehicles or long commutes, towing coverage can offset the cost of an unexpected breakdown.

## Glass and Windshield Coverage

Glass coverage addresses damage to your windshield, windows, and other glass surfaces, which are among the most frequently damaged parts of a vehicle. On most policies, glass damage falls under comprehensive coverage, meaning your comprehensive deductible would normally apply to a repair or replacement. Some insurers offer full glass coverage or a waived deductible for small chip repairs, since fixing a chip early can prevent a larger and more expensive crack later. Prompt attention to glass damage helps maintain safety and visibility while keeping repair costs down.

## When These Add-Ons Make Sense

These convenience coverages are generally inexpensive, but their value depends on your driving habits, vehicle age, and personal preferences. A driver with a newer vehicle under warranty may already have roadside service through the manufacturer, while someone with an older car might benefit from adding it to the policy. Reviewing whether you already have similar benefits through a membership or credit card can prevent paying twice for the same service. Weighing the small premium against the potential inconvenience of a breakdown helps you decide what to include.
