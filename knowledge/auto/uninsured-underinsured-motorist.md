# Uninsured and Underinsured Motorist Coverage

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage protects you when the driver responsible for an accident cannot fully pay for the harm they caused. Even though liability insurance is required in most places, some drivers carry no coverage, carry too little, or flee the scene entirely. This coverage steps into the gap left by the at-fault party so that you are not stuck paying for someone else's mistake. It is a valuable complement to liability coverage because it protects you rather than the other driver.

## Uninsured Motorist Coverage

Uninsured motorist coverage applies when the at-fault driver has no liability insurance at all, or in many states when a hit-and-run driver cannot be identified. It generally covers medical expenses, lost wages, and other injury-related costs for you and your passengers, similar to the bodily injury coverage the other driver should have carried. Some states also offer an uninsured motorist property damage option that helps pay to repair your vehicle. Because the number of uninsured drivers can be significant in some areas, this coverage provides meaningful peace of mind.

## Underinsured Motorist Coverage

Underinsured motorist coverage applies when the at-fault driver does carry insurance, but their limits are too low to cover the full cost of your injuries or damages. For example, if your medical bills reach 60,000 dollars but the other driver's policy caps out at 25,000, this coverage can help pay the difference up to your own limits. It ensures that a serious accident does not leave you personally responsible for expenses caused by another person's inadequate coverage. This protection is increasingly important as medical and repair costs continue to rise.

## Bodily Injury and Property Damage Components

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is often split into bodily injury and property damage components, though availability varies by state. The bodily injury portion focuses on medical costs, lost income, and related expenses for people in your vehicle. The property damage portion, where offered, helps repair your car when the responsible driver lacks sufficient coverage. Reviewing which components are available and required in your state helps you build a complete safety net.

## Choosing Your Limits

Many insurance professionals recommend matching your uninsured and underinsured motorist limits to your liability limits so your protection is balanced. Higher limits cost more but provide stronger coverage in the event of a severe accident with an uninsured or underinsured driver. Some states let you decline or reduce this coverage in writing, while others require it as a standard part of every policy. Considering the prevalence of uninsured drivers in your region can help you decide how much protection is appropriate.
